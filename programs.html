Qualification & Practice

Four stages, one standard: the actuarial examination pathway.

From foundational mathematics to specialist practice, the Institute's examinations mirror the rigour expected of internationally recognised actuarial bodies, adapted to the Ugandan regulatory context.

The Pathway

From Student to Fellow

  1. Foundational — Core Mathematics & Statistics

    Probability, financial mathematics and statistical modelling. Typically completed alongside an accredited undergraduate degree at Makerere, Kyambogo, MUST or UCU.

  2. Core Actuarial Principles

    Life contingencies, risk modelling and the economic principles underlying insurance and pension mathematics.

  3. Core Practice — Associateship

    Application of actuarial techniques to real-world reserving, pricing and capital problems. Successful candidates are admitted as Associates (AIA).

  4. Specialist Practice — Fellowship

    Candidates choose a specialism — life insurance, general insurance, or retirement & pensions — and sit a case-study examination reflecting live market conditions. Fellows (FIA) may sign statutory actuarial reports.

Continuing Professional Development

Staying current is a condition of membership

Structured CPD

Formal courses, conferences and Institute-run seminars count toward at least 15 of the 30 annual hours required of Fellows in practice.

Unstructured CPD

Technical reading, mentoring and committee work may account for the remainder, subject to a reflective log submitted annually.

Audit & Compliance

A sample of members is audited each year; shortfalls trigger a remediation plan before renewal of the practising certificate.
